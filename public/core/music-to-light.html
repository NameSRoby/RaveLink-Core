Recipe, color roles & timing

One complete recipe is chosen and locked when activated. Audio drives its response; it never chooses or changes the recipe. Artistic edits apply to the next activation. Blocking a selected layer or scenario ends the current recipe and restores its lights. Rave also needs granted audio access, a selected source and allowed music lights. Rave draws one scenario and optional layers once per request, or follows your pinned choices. Blocked layers are never drawn. The Preview / Go Live mode is separate. Song timing ends at the current song's end; with one minute or less remaining, it covers the following song too. Pauses hold the song timer. Missing timing uses the dynamic-effect timer (or the default above when unset). Separate music timer uses the seconds above instead. Lost song timing falls back to a finite timer. Every song effect has a 30-minute safety limit.

Calibration & output settings

The highest output your lights may use. Intensity stays within this limit.

Play a representative passage at your usual comfortable volume. Calibration captures the selected source in Preview, never changes playback volume and never controls lights. It sets a fixed reference; quiet passages stay quiet.

Calibration options · 30 seconds with 3 dB headroom

Include both quieter and louder passages. A short quiet sample can make later music sit at the ceiling and lose detail. If calibrated loudness stays at 100%, use a longer sample or more headroom. The reference stays fixed after calibration.

If changing app volume changes the captured meter, recalibrate. Automatic volume compensation requires a verified gain reading; headphone loudness cannot be inferred from a volume percentage.

Hue Entertainment uses the selected rate after connection preheat. Hue REST shares an 8-command/second budget. WiZ can update independently up to the selected rate. Adaptive output uses 2–8 Hz for calibrated energy, 12 Hz for rises and up to 16 Hz for strong rises or attacks, capped by your selection. The fast-rate hold prevents chatter. Slow writes drop intermediate frames instead of building a backlog. These are requested rates, not guaranteed physical bulb refresh rates.
